Minutes 8 to 20 · Task 1A and 1B

Part A: the written text

Read the article once all the way through (about 4 minutes). Then read it again with the questions in mind. The paragraphs are numbered so you can refer to them.

Text A: "The Basket by the Door"

An opinion column from a family lifestyle magazine

1Last spring, I counted. Over one Sunday dinner, the five people at our table picked up their phones forty-one times. I know because I was one of them. Eleven of those forty-one were mine.

2Nobody was being rude, exactly. My son was checking a football score. My daughter was replying to a group chat about homework. My husband was "just looking something up" to settle an argument about the height of the Eiffel Tower. We were together in the same room, sharing the same lasagne, and yet each of us was somewhere else.

3Have you ever spoken to someone and watched their eyes drift down to a glowing rectangle? It is a small thing. It lasts a second. But it says, quietly and clearly: you are not the most interesting thing here.

4So we bought a basket. A cheap wicker one, the kind you might keep apples in. We put it on the shelf by the front door, and we made one rule: when you walk in for dinner, your phone goes in the basket.

5The first week was a disaster. My daughter sulked. My son claimed he might miss "something important" (it was a video of a cat falling off a sofa). I caught myself reaching for my pocket like a smoker reaching for a cigarette that is not there. The silence at the table felt heavy and awkward, like a room after an argument.

6Then, slowly, something changed. In the third week, my son told us a long, ridiculous story about his chemistry teacher. In the fourth week, my daughter asked her grandmother how she met my father, and we sat there for an hour. Nobody looked up the answer. Nobody needed to.

7I am not against technology. My phone lets me see my sister's face every week, even though she lives four thousand kilometres away. But a tool that connects us to people far away can also disconnect us from the people beside us.

8The basket is still there. Some evenings it overflows with chargers and cables. Some evenings someone "forgets" and the rule is quietly broken. But most nights, for one hour, we are only where we are.

9Try it. Find a basket. You might be surprised by what you hear when the screens go dark.

Worked example: use this as a model

Question: Explain why the writer includes the detail about her sister in paragraph 7.

Model answer: The writer mentions that her phone lets her see her sister "every week" to show that she values technology. This makes her argument seem fair and balanced, because she admits phones have benefits. As a result, readers are more likely to trust her and follow her advice.

Pattern: point (what she does), evidence (short quotation), effect (what it shows or how it affects the reader).

Minutes 28 to 45 · Task 1E practice

Part C: compare and contrast

In the real eAssessment, Task 1E asks you to write a full compare-and-contrast essay. Today you practise the structure. Click each underlined space and type. Where you see options in [square brackets], choose one or write your own.

Essay question: Compare and contrast how Text A ("The Basket by the Door") and Text B ("I Forgot My Phone") present the effect of smartphones on human connection.

Before you write: the comparison grid (3 minutes)

Text A: the articleText B: the film
Model grid

Message: A: phones pull families apart, but small rules can fix it. B: phones make us absent from our own lives.

Evidence: A: "each of us was somewhere else". B: the birthday boy filming his own party.

Tone: A: reflective, then hopeful. B: humorous at moments, mostly bleak and critical.

Ending: A: a call to action ("Try it. Find a basket."). B: back in bed, partner still on his phone.

Effect: A: readers feel encouraged to act. B: viewers feel uncomfortable and recognise their own habits.

Useful linking words: both, similarly, likewise, in the same way, however, in contrast, whereas, while, on the other hand, unlike, therefore, as a result, ultimately.
